A Tunnel Through Extinction





This tunnel book takes inspiration from the iconic “rabbit hole” spread in Alice in Wonderland pop-up books which is a visual metaphor for falling through space and meaning. But instead of chasing a white rabbit, this takes viewers on a descent through the Five Mass Extinctions in Earth’s history, arriving finally in the present day.

Each layer represents one mass extinction event:

Ordovician-Silurian Extinction

Late Devonian Extinction

Permian-Triassic Extinction

Triassic-Jurassic Extinction

Cretaceous-Paleogene Extinction

Final layer: the Anthropocene, a modern city skyline at night

The colors transition from vibrant to subdued, mirroring the progressive loss of biodiversity and vitality. Early layers are dominated by bright greens and organic browns. Later layers shift into muted purples, deep blues, and finally harsh artificial lights, reflecting urban modernity’s alienation from nature.

Each extinction layer is labeled.

A hidden Easter egg: the black structure with red grid on the final layer is the LSE Centre Building (CBG)…

From a distance, the book reads as an abstract landscape. But up close, details emerge, therefore encouraging the viewer to linger, lean in, and time-travel through geological memory.

The final layer of the tunnel book also allows for the insertion of a photograph. Whether it’s an image of a cityscape, a familiar building, or a fragment of everyday life, this photo will serve as a visual reminder that the Anthropocene is not abstract: it is now, and it is us.

The final layer also allows for the insertion of a mirror, letting viewers to catch a faint glimpse of their own reflection as they peer through the tunnel. The viewer therefore becomes part of the composition, a living figure framed within the sixth extinction layer.
