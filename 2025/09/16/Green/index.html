绿 | Green

中文版

吕缕最喜欢妈妈。那是一段现在想来全是光斑的记忆。早上的第一个触感是妈妈手心的温热，牵着，慢慢睁开眼，先看天花板上的花纹，再一点点挪向窗外刺眼的光。脸颊被捏捏，胳肢窝被挠挠，她就会咯吱咯吱笑，妈妈也会笑起来。

现在吕缕不知道了。她醒来先是望见桌上成堆的书。走出门，爸爸瘦削的身影一晃就只剩下门关上的砰砰响。只剩下她和妈妈。只剩下，一个问题：老师在群里发班级成绩了吗？

她故作轻松地坐下。没有爸爸的存在，房间里即刻安静得可怕。馒头干巴巴的。她揪下一块，推给不存在的小精灵。

“我和老师打过报告了，你早上不用去学校了。”

吕缕身体僵硬了。馒头捏在手里，她盯着空荡荡的碗。

随之而来的是身体摔倒在地。早上的第一个触感，是妈妈给的巴掌的冰冷。然后是第二个第三个，伴随两人撕心裂肺的哭声。

“黄雪萍又是第一！你怎么这么，这么不成器，废物，生你有什么用，有什么用，数学考那么低……“

馒头被捏得汗津津的，小小地黏在手心里。闭上眼前，她看见窗外的光，顺着水泥地砖爬过来，透骨奇寒。

吕缕喜欢黄雪萍，她们是一辈子的最好的朋友。这是经过拉勾上吊一百年不许变的保证的。她们手牵手跳皮筋，大课间结束给老师送作业，午休时翻花绳。日复一日，雷打不动。黄雪萍会记得她的小精灵，吃饭时会给隐形的精灵塞点饭粒。黄雪萍还会说要给精灵摘石榴吃，她会教吕缕怎么爬树，两人一起坐在石榴树上，对面夕阳溏心蛋黄，滚烫。

在瑞蓝小学，地域原因，很多人姓黄，但黄雪萍是特别的。几乎所有老师和同学认识她。她几乎永远是第一名，隔三差五就要在国旗下演讲和领奖，还是大队长，校啦啦队队长，获得过全国少儿戏曲最高奖项。她的父母都大字不识，而瑞蓝是个小得不得了的偏僻山村，这就显得她的优秀在众人眼中更弥足珍贵起来。

吕缕经常抱着妈妈的手机反复欣赏“小兰花”赛场的表演回放。黄雪萍的五官大而立体，眼睛仿佛是两颗亮晶晶的荔枝核，嗓音也宽亮脆甜。她带着不属于这个年纪的真挚感情投入进去，咿咿呀呀，行云流水。

那天黄雪萍只是坐在吕缕家里看电视。她无意间点到中央戏曲频道，霎时就被吸引了。吕缕看不懂戏，觉得黄雪萍甚是奇怪。再过几天，黄雪萍斩钉截铁对她说“我要学唱戏”。显然她也是这样对她妈妈说的。吕缕仍然奇怪，因为她还从来没有过像这样冥冥中注定特别想做一件事的时刻。

黄雪萍好厉害。如果没有黄雪萍，自己就不会被妈妈打了。吕缕有时想。妈妈爸爸都是大学毕业的理科生，村里最有学问的人。看到黄雪萍有那样没文化的父母还如此聪敏，自己孩子却数学都不及格，根本不知道是像谁，妈妈就会很生气。爸爸阻拦也无济于事。

吕缕承载了妈妈的愤怒，不知道放哪。她的题目被眼泪打得糊成一团。她随便写下几串数字，和妈妈说提前写完了一周的作业，妈妈终于开心起来。作业本就这样变成定时炸弹，妈妈什么时候批改，什么时候完蛋。吕缕学会了拖延，像黄雪萍的赌鬼爸爸，先借钱，等讨债的什么时候找上门再说。她下楼看见坐在石榴树上的黄雪萍，转身逃跑了，风呼啸过耳畔，她都跑到瑞蓝河去了，却好像还不够。她一拳打在岸边柳树上。抠着树皮发呆了很久。

小孩其实分不清愤怒和害怕。她以为自己是生黄雪萍的气，实际是她看到她会想起被打时的恐惧。更别说忮忌这种情绪。

无论如何，黄雪萍的阴影从未离去。

吕缕进入了瑞蓝中学，黄雪萍考入了市最好的绿沁中学。吕缕的成绩依旧一言难尽。和小学唯一不同的是她不再在草稿纸上画画了，只呆滞地转笔。小精灵也彻底消失了。除非体育课要跑步，不然身体一点也不想动。吕缕饭吃不下，头晕目眩，窗外的绿叶断断续续，终于合上两天没睡的眼。

简单来说，她想报复社会。人生望不到头，如此黑暗，不如全都毁灭。她又跑到瑞蓝河去，想投毒的可能性。河好长，分割开她和对面的村庄。水里刚被放生的乌龟由于不会游泳，溺死了。小时候，爸爸钓鱼，她在一旁会把鱼饵包进柳叶里，下饺子一样丢进河里，盼望有鱼能吃到天降美食。那时柳叶包的小球也是这样沉入河底。现在她只想让所有人享有无边无际的死亡。

投毒的话，需要有很大剂量才能保证有效。她放弃了这个想法，转而抱着对病毒的兴趣，开始学习化学。

先是化学成绩，然后是物理、数学、英语……

最后，是妈妈对她说：“你小学时候的那个黄雪萍，成绩一落千丈，她爸妈现在天天打她哟。也不让她唱戏，就让她老老实实走文化路线，想要她考状元！你可得加油啊。”

吕缕的手颤抖起来，抠着胳膊上的疤，心里是一股怒火和快感。她想冲去黄雪萍家，把她爸妈大打一顿，想质问黄雪萍和妈妈，话到嘴边又不知说什么。只能听见心脏跳动。

之后很久没有童年玩伴的消息。吕缕因为成绩越来越好，所有人都对她和颜悦色起来。当她收到绿沁高中录取通知书时，对社会的怨恨都消失了。妈妈也不打了，逢人便说吕缕是学习的料。总之，一切都时来运转，蒸蒸日上，欣欣向荣。

最终吕缕考上了亚洲最好的贵淳大学，和父母一样是工程专业，家里又有足够的钱送她去这所学校，简直顺水推舟得不能再顺。

在遥远的贵淳，她有了女朋友，还和同学们组建了一支支教队伍。意外的是，学校选择了让他们前往瑞蓝小学支教。

某天，吕缕在一位小孩的试卷上出附加题。小孩要写“没学会钓鱼”，她就画了一张示意图，鱼竿掉了，鱼沉下去，旁边是眼泪哗哗的哭脸，想他看图说话。他却写：“没有钓到鱼，我很生气。”这如何是生气呢？她甚是奇怪。

然后，她想起来了。小时候的认知是，看到嘴角下垂，就是妈妈生气了，不是吗？妈妈掉下的眼泪，不是出于悲伤，是愤懑，难道不是吗？

再见黄雪萍，在瑞蓝河岸。

吕缕的身形和她爸爸一样瘦削，支教的红马甲松松垮垮挂在肩上，像一盏警示灯。黄雪萍一双眼睛黯然无光地向前看，被她吓了一跳。

“其实……我还没考上本科呢。复读两年了。“重逢闲聊间，黄雪萍很不好意思道。

吕缕嗫嚅着安慰几句。

回到家。脱下马甲，盯着一桌子肥鱼大肉，沉默。

爸爸还没下班。但家里仍然是热闹的了。等妈妈坐下来，她抬头：“黄雪萍还没考上本科。”

妈妈也沉默了。

随后两人同时大笑起来。笑得几乎要把这辈子的苦闷都吐出。吕缕感觉自己从来没有发出过这么大的声响。笑得像哭，喉咙发紧，挤出不成形的“嗬嗬”声。黄雪萍，你也有今天！她前仰后合，语无伦次。两人都十分癫狂，完全不知道该看哪里，该对谁倾诉，只能一抽一抽地蠕动。幸灾乐祸。

吕缕突然停下来。奇怪地看着妈妈。

窗外，明月高悬，却和童年的阳光一样刺眼。

English Ver.

Lü Lü loved Mommy best. Looking back now, those mornings are all specks of light. The first thing she felt each day was Mommy’s warm palm, holding hers, easing her eyes open: ceiling patterns first, then inch by inch to the stabbing glare outside the window. A cheek pinch, a little tickle under the arms, and she would giggle and giggle; Mommy would laugh too.

Now, Lü Lü isn’t sure anymore. She wakes to towers of books on the desk. She steps out; her father’s thin frame flickers past, and then only the double thud of the door. It is just her and her mother. Just one question: has the teacher posted the class scores?

She sits as if at ease. Without her father, the room is suddenly, frighteningly still. The bun is dry. She tears off a piece and slides it toward the little sprite that isn’t there.

“I’ve spoken with your teacher. You don’t have to go to school this morning.”

Lü Lü’s body locks. The bun sweats in her palm. She stares at the empty bowl.

Then she is on the floor. The first touch of the morning is the cold of her mother’s slap. Then a second, a third, while both of them cry as if their throats are tearing.

“Huang Xueping is first again! Why are you so—so useless, a waste—what good are you, what good—failing math…”

The bun, damp now, sticks in small crumbs to her hand. With her eyes shut, she sees the light outside crawling across the cement tiles, cold to the bone.

Lü Lü liked Huang Xueping. They were best friends for life. That was sealed with pinky swears, with promises sworn never to break for a hundred years. They jumped rope hand in hand; when recess ended, they carried homework to the teachers; at lunch they wove string figures. Day after day, rain or shine. Huang Xueping remembered her little sprite, slipping grains of rice to the invisible guest at meals. She even said she would pluck pomegranates for the sprite, and she taught Lü Lü how to climb. The two of them perched together in the branches, the sunset across the river a molten yolk, blazing hot.

At Ruilan Elementary, many children shared the surname Huang, but Huang Xueping was different. Nearly every teacher and student knew her. She was almost always first in class, often called to speak and collect awards beneath the national flag. She was squad leader, captain of the cheer team, even winner of the country’s top prize for children’s opera: Little Orchid. Her parents could not read a single character, yet in a village as small and remote as Ruilan, her brilliance seemed all the more precious.

Lü Lü often curled up with Mommy’s phone, replaying the Little Orchid competition. Huang Xueping’s features were bold and striking, her eyes like two shining lychee seeds. Her voice was wide and bright and sweet, carrying a passion far beyond her years, notes flowing as if without effort.

It had begun so simply: one afternoon at Lü Lü’s house, the TV tuned by chance to the opera channel, and she was transfixed. Lü Lü did not understand opera and was confused. A few days later, Huang Xueping declared, “I want to learn to sing opera.” Surely she had told her mother the same. Lü Lü was still confused: she had never felt that kind of fated desire to do something, as if it had been chosen for her.

Huang Xueping was astonishing. If not for her, Lü Lü sometimes thought, Mommy wouldn’t have beaten her. Mommy and Daddy were both college graduates, the most educated in the village. To see Huang Xueping, with illiterate parents, so quick and gifted, while their own child could not even pass math, made Mommy furious. Daddy’s protests meant nothing.

Lü Lü carried Mommy’s rage, with nowhere to set it down. Tears blurred her homework into stains. She scrawled numbers at random, then told her mother she had finished a week’s work ahead of time. At last Mommy was pleased. The exercise book became a time bomb, waiting for the day Mommy would check. That was when everything would explode. Lü Lü learned to stall, like Huang Xueping’s gambling father, borrowing first, waiting for the debt collector to come.

One afternoon she went downstairs and saw Huang Xueping on the pomegranate tree. She turned and fled, wind tearing past her ears. She ran all the way to the Ruilan River, and still it was not far enough. She punched the bark of a willow at the bank. Then sat picking at it, staring blankly for a long time.

Children cannot really tell anger from fear. She thought she was angry at Huang Xueping, but in truth, seeing her only awakened the terror of being beaten. Jealousy was something else entirely, too complicated to name.

Still, Huang Xueping’s shadow never left.

Lü Lü went on to Ruilan Middle School, while Huang Xueping entered Greenvale, the best in the city. Lü Lü’s grades remained abysmal. The only difference from elementary school was that she no longer filled scrap paper with drawings, only spinning her pen blankly. The sprite was gone for good. Unless she had to run in P.E., the body refused to move. Lü Lü could not eat. The room spun. Beyond the window, leaves fractured and stitched themselves again, until her sleepless eyes, after two days, surrendered to the dark.

In short, she wanted to take revenge on the world. If life stretched on only into darkness, then let it all be destroyed. She went again to the Ruilan River and wondered how one might poison it. The river was long, dividing her from the villages across. A turtle, freed into the river, sank and drowned as it did not know how to swim. As a child, while her father fished, she had wrapped bait in willow leaves and dropped them in like dumplings, hoping fish would taste such heaven-sent food. The little balls sank then as they did now. Now she only wanted everyone to share in an endless death.

But poison would require such a great dose to be sure. She abandoned the thought, turned instead to a fascination with viruses, and started to study chemistry.

First chemistry scores rose, then physics, then math, then English……

And at last, her mother said: “That Huang Xueping from your elementary days, her grades have collapsed. Her parents beat her every day now. They’ve forbidden her to sing opera, made her stay on the academic track. They want her to be the top scorer! You’d better keep working hard.”

Lü Lü’s hand trembled. She clawed at the scar on her arm, anger and pleasure flooding together. She wanted to storm into Huang Xueping’s house and thrash her parents, to confront Huang Xueping and her mother, but when the words reached her lips, nothing came out. All she heard was heart beats.

After that she heard nothing of her childhood friend for a long time. But as Lü Lü’s grades climbed, everyone grew kind to her. When she received her admission to Greenvale High, all her hatred for society evaporated. Her mother stopped beating her, boasted everywhere that Lü Lü was made for study. Everything turned, at last: time’s wheel rolling forward, bright and unstoppable.

In the end, Lü Lü entered Guichun University, the finest in Yeltan, to study engineering like her parents. The family had money enough to send her, and so everything smooth, as if set on rails.

In distant Guichun, she found a girlfriend, and with peers she formed a volunteer teaching team. By chance, the school sent them back to Ruilan Elementary.

One day, on a child’s test paper, she was adding an extra exercise: the boy was to describe “not learning how to fish.” She drew him a picture: rod dropped, fish sinking, beside it a face streaming with tears, hoping he would spin a story from the image. Instead he wrote: “I didn’t catch the fish. I was angry.” Angry? How could this be anger? she was confused.

And then she remembered. As a child, she had thought a mouth turned down meant anger, hadn’t it? And when Mommy’s tears fell, were they not born not of sorrow, but of resentment? Wasn’t that what she had always believed?

Seeing Huang Xueping again was on the banks of the Ruilan River.

Lü Lü’s frame was as thin as her father’s, the red vest of the teaching team hanging loose on her shoulders like a warning light. Huang Xueping’s eyes, dulled and vacant, startled as they met her.

“Actually…… I still haven’t gotten into college. I’ve repeated two years now,” she admitted, embarrassed, in the drift of small talk.

Lü Lü stammered a few words of comfort.

Back home, she slipped off the vest and stared in silence at the table heavy with fish and meat.

Her father had not yet come back from work. Still, the house was already loud with life. When her mother sat down, she lifted her head: “Huang Xueping still hasn’t gotten into college.”

Her mother fell silent too.

And then the two of them burst out laughing. Laughing as if to expel a lifetime of suffocation. Lü Lü felt she had never made such a sound before. The laughter scraped her throat, came out ragged like sobs. Huang Xueping, so this is your turn! She doubled over, incoherent. Both were half-mad, unable to fix their gaze, unable to confide, their bodies rippling in broken spasms. Schadenfreude.

Lü Lü stopped suddenly, staring at her mother in confusion.

Outside, the moon hung high, but shone as harshly as the sunlight of childhood.

A/N

写的时候看见吕缕和黄雪萍跳皮筋，想到自己小时候从来不会跳。不仅跳皮筋，翻花绳、手指游戏什么的也不会（就是开智晚晚的呵呵）。最喜欢做的就是一个人跳房子。感觉吕缕和黄雪萍都是小学时超级机灵的孩子呢。

我是从朋友那里听来的这个故事，因为实在太有“既定的宿命感”所以感慨万千后决定写下来，并对原本的结局进行了改编。吕缕原型其实从头到尾都没有对黄雪萍原型产生过任何恨意或者因为这样的挫折就像故事结尾一样疯掉，而且是很励志的。这里我是全凭想象在朋友故事的基础上塑造了吕缕和黄雪萍两位人物，几乎和原型没什么关系了（包括性格等等）。

在写之前，我必须先代入吕缕，才能尽量保证真实。这无疑是痛苦的，我必须假设我是另一个人，用不同年龄段的视角去写作，但我觉得结果很值得。

在吕缕进入高中后节奏明显变快了。比如瑞蓝河、贵淳、女朋友什么的，其实是想要制造一种信息量上的增加，显得“黄雪萍”似乎已经过去很久，生活中有了不少新事物。最后回归黄雪萍，母女两人仍然在家中，仍然在笑，好像什么都没变。

几乎每个伏笔都有进行回收，爱抚与巴掌的对比、阳光、小精灵、溺河的隐喻、工程对应理科等等。我在尝试新写作题材，我以前从未写过亲子和童年创伤相关（之前都是环境正义之类），我也很久没写虚构写作了。

绿色象征忮忌，而绿又与“吕缕”谐音。而且，我还打算写另外两篇分别为《红》和《蓝》的虚构小说，所以这篇名为《绿》正正好。

我顺带修了Auto模式时网页界面是浅色字体和白色背景但底部又是黑色的bug。

When I was writing, I pictured Lü Lü and Huang Xueping playing jump rope, and it struck me that I myself never knew how to play as a child. Not just jump rope, I couldn’t do string figures or hand games either (a sign of slow childhood development, I suppose). What I loved most was playing hopscotch alone. Lü Lü and Huang Xueping, in contrast, both felt like the kind of the cleverest of kids in elementary school.

I first heard this story from a friend. It carried such a strong sense of predestined fate that I felt compelled to write it down by rendering this sense even more inescapable, so I altered the original ending. In reality, the prototype for Lü Lü never once felt hatred toward the prototype for Huang Xueping, nor did setbacks ever drive her to madness as in my fictional ending. Quite the opposite, hers was a very inspiring story. My version of Lü Lü and Huang Xueping was shaped almost entirely by imagination on top of my friend’s account, with little connection to their real-life counterparts (including personalities and so forth).

Before writing, I had to first immerse myself in Lü Lü’s perspective to ensure some degree of authenticity. That process was painful because I had to assume I was another person entirely, and shift between different ages and viewpoints while writing. Still, I believe the result was worth it.

Once Lü Lü entered high school, the pace of the narrative clearly quickened. Mentions of the Ruilan River, Guichun, the girlfriend, and so on were deliberate attempts to create a sense of increased information flow, suggesting that “Huang Xueping” already belonged to the past, while life had brought many new things. Yet in the end, it circles back to Huang Xueping: mother and daughter, still at home, still laughing, as though nothing had changed.

Nearly every motif was revisited: the contrast between caress and slap, the sunlight, the little sprite, the metaphor of drowning in the river, the engineering/science connection, and so on. I was experimenting with new territory. I had never written about parent–child relationships or childhood trauma before (my past work dealt more with environmental justice). It had also been a long time since I last attempted fiction.

Green symbolizes envy, and it also sounds the same as the name “Lü Lü” (in Chinese). I also plan to write two companion pieces, Red and Blue, so this one is aptly titled Green.
