Becoming Waste

Cloud bastions stack before the stained-glass window, blocking panes already as bland as plain water. Behind them the black frames delineate the (clouds) as a sooty mass, a vast, fluffy tail sweeping the threshold. Dread sweeps this city. The rain-soaked, pitch-black roadway being eroded, the sky is duller than usual, gloom diffuses. It coils among the ordinarily tedious sculptures, the exercise apparatus, the streetlamps, and so on. The shell of St Thomas’s Hospital is hard, a pallid sigh slides down, flowing for eternity.

The strokes of graffiti are like spiders’ webs. Ju lowers his hand, smoke seeps from mouth and nose. A shower cap, a ball of steel wool, at last becoming a white flower drifting upward. He lets his head droop, corpse-like, over the rotted roots of wild greens. That rectangular prism which cannot be called a chair, only such burlap is left. He thinks of this cloth and that, of stains, of black dough. When he raises his eyes again, the sky is already leaden. So leaden that everything is reduced to blurred shadow. R-r-rumble. Maybe workers are dragging refuse bins on the far side of the tunnel, perhaps the antiquated tube is passing overhead, or else, another downpour is about to arrive…

Ju listens without expression, as the sky grows darker and darker. In the angle between the hospital and the overpass above the tunnel there had once formed a bolt of gold-bright satin, endlessly heaving. It kept itself alive, barely, under the city’s tailoring.

Then he looks back once; his heart, unusually, gives a leap. He can scarcely recognize his own shadow. It stands there like a monster, the head welded to the shoulders at an uncanny junction. As if it were another person, lifeless, staring until his skin crawls. All at once it appears, face to face.
