About

This website is a derivative work based on my life. The creators of my life are not responsible for it in any way.

Updated on 2025-05-01
a 18-year-old’s server

This website was created in 2021 when I was 15 years old. It began simply as an archive, a quiet corner to store (a bunch of nonsense stuff I wrote). The name “doradolive” traces back to a creature I imagined in a middle school novel titled Arcturus: ocellrado, a species that embodied the unknown and the uncontrollable.

Originally, it held 47 archives—but over time, I found myself unsatisfied with most of what I wrote back in high school. So I removed many of those early pieces, and added new ones from my university days.

If I had to tell my story, I’d say—it’s kind of simple. Before I started my undergrad study in LSE, I hadn’t studied human geography or the social sciences (and had to take my secondary school geography exam twice just to pass). I didn’t grow up in a world of theory or critical writing. My high school days were filled with equations, experiments, and problem sets: I studied further mathematics, chemistry, physics, and economics. I learned how to trace systems, how to follow rules, how to solve for X.

I didn’t attend one of the big-name international schools. I came from a small public international school in Nanjing, the kind of place where resources were limited but curiosity can survive. English isn’t my first language; I’ve only been in the UK for a year. And before this, I’d never been formally trained to write academic essays—no critical essays, no argumentative writing.

But these early paths also left something useful. Proofs and derivatives taught me to hold precision close, to distrust easy answers. Economics slipped in the idea that behind every system, there’s a question of justice.

So when I finally arrived university, I started learning new words for old instincts: structure, power, narrative, consequence. And now, this website is a place where those threads gather.
