About

But the sun is, at every moment, both a setting sun and a rising one.

This website hosts a curated selection of writings. The central aim is to think more precisely, and feel more honestly, about the entanglements between environment and society. Works cross disciplinary boundaries such as sociology, human geography, political ecology, environmental economics, and quantitative reasoning, examining how our conceptual tools shape what we are capable of seeing. It documents an ongoing attempt to understand a world that is often contradictory and emotionally overwhelming.

If you are new here, there is no required order. The simplest way is to start from the most recent posts. Updates are irregular.

A Brief History of doradolive

doradolive was first created in 2021, when I was fifteen and teaching myself how to build a static website on GitHub. With no formal training in computer science and long before AI tools became widely available, I assembled the site through trial and error.

The name “doradolive” traces back to an imaginary species I created in a middle-school novel, Arcturus. The creature, ocellrado, embodied the unknown and the uncontrollable and the name was later adapted as the domain for this site.

Between 2021 and 2024, the site held roughly forty-seven entries. Many were written during high school, before I had any formal exposure to social sciences such as human geography. As my academic interests shifted, I removed a large portion of these early pieces and began adding new ones from my undergraduate work.

About the Author

I am an undergraduate student at the London School of Economics and Political Science, studying BSc Environment and Sustainable Development with Economics in the Geography Department. I did mathematics, further mathematics, physics, chemistry, and economics for my A-Levels. I twice failed middle-school geography. Most of what appears on this site grows out of this trajectory.
