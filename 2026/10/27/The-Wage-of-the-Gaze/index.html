Academic Reflection
October 27, 2026

The Wage of the Gaze

A noisy billboard. I thought. Oversaturated, texts too big, whole thing overcrowded, too many fish in the tank. I caught my own reflection in it, and a version of me that had split off from the first suddenly admired it as a perfect billboard, the sci-fi palette, the 6:3:1 composition, the clean signage. I wanted it so badly! Then a wave of nausea. Is that you? Is the billboard you?

We usually cannot tell whether a person is envying or despising, observing someone else or reflecting oneself. This is similar to what Arnesen charged against whiteness studies, that the analysis is “mental”, that you cannot get inside the worker’s head, so any claim about what he was really feeling can never be proved. In this blog I want to take that charge and try to turn it over.

The individual

Eric Lott studied the songs, the performances and the box-office numbers of blackface minstrelsy, treating form as the outward showing of an inner state. Roediger (as discussed in GY315 Week 5) works the same by pointing to minstrel songs that asked white audiences to take the blackface performer as Black and to side with him against Southern whites, songs with lines like “join de Black Brigade”, alongside Henry Clay Work’s hugely popular numbers that celebrated the South’s defeat, where the freed slave sings “We’ll be de Massa, he’ll be de servant.” It also carries a fantasy of cross-racial identification and class solidarity, though reading that as genuine solidarity may romanticise it into a common cause, since the autonomist composes more fellowship into the scene.

The engine of this blog, I would call it, is fascination, or ambivalence. Homi Bhabha described the colonial gaze as desire and derision locked together, and his formula for mimicry, “almost the same, but not quite”, is similar to what I want to discuss. Envy and contempt both come out of one prior state, an unsettled fascination that has not resolved into a verdict, and they are simply the two directions it can resolve in. Thus, the same billboard makes one person want to buy and another feel sick because a single fascination has two exits.

You can see the same structure in sexuality. Judith Butler argued that heterosexuality constitutes itself by expelling the desire it disowns, so the straight gaze at the queer wears the same face of love and theft. I would rather follow Siobhan Somerville, whose Queering the Color Line shows that the hetero/homo line and the black/white line were manufactured by one apparatus at the same historical moment, through the same vocabulary of mixture and degeneration. Her co-construction does more work than a parallel analogy, since it lets Butler stay in the background and lets me drop the more decorative appeals to abjection altogether.

In addition: form proves structure, however, and it does not prove function. Showing that a song is ambivalent is a different thing from showing that the ambivalence bought the worker’s compliance (one is representation and the other is redistribution).

The society

Now pull the lens back from individuals’ gaze to the question of how an identity gets made at all. Karen and Barbara Fields, in Racecraft, argue that racism comes first and race is its product, which kills the question I kept returning to, how a society could have less comparison in it. Since a relational term has no existence without its contrast, there is nothing there to subtract.

I have three cases to make this same structure visible. In the American material the projected content is bound to industrial labour discipline, and it runs downward, from the dominant group onto the subordinate one. In Sono Sion’s Guilty of Romance the subjects change completely, since what is repressed organises around family, reproduction and the postwar gender order, making it a gendered and sexual formation. In Rome it’s about civilisational standing and imperial legitimacy. The military victor, secure in his power, projects onto the people he has conquered the very refinement he feels he lacks, which is what Horace meant when he wrote that captured Greece took her savage conqueror captive. Projection therefore flows toward the axis on which the dominant party feels a lack, and not necessarily the axis on which he dominates.

Affect is universal, while politics is situated, and the situatedness lives entirely in the content, the direction and the function, with none of it living in the affect.

The mechanism

W. E. B. Du Bois described a public and psychological wage paid to white workers, a compensation in status and superiority that stood in for better material conditions. That wage is fascination and fascination itself is the wage before it has been paid. A society pays this wage in order to settle the unresolved fascination and lock it into a single direction.

The deconstructive account thinks identity is empty and purely relational (a claim about its origin), while the materialist account thinks it issues a positive wage (a claim about its effect). Wage, is the machine that sediments the first into the second. Wage, is the connection in between representation and redistribution.

The decolonial question then becomes sharper, though it does not thereby become answered: whether we can have fascination without the wage that settles it into hierarchy.

The analyst

If we pull the lens back onto the person holding the camera, we can take Guilty of Romance as an example again. The commonality between Mitsuko and Izumi is pushed to its absolute limit, ‘her blood runs into my blood’, and the ending is mutual destruction instead of care. The hope would run that I see your situation as worse than mine and am led toward concern instead of exclusion, which is easily dismantled, since fascination tends toward destruction even when the commonality is total.

However I have to say reflexivity is not an alibi. If it goes into the claim that every gaze is fascinated and therefore no one can criticise anyone, that is the everywhere trap again. Reflexivity, as always, requires the one judging to account for the position his own reading obscures or brings to light. Writing this blog, I am also distributing that attention.

The same doubt returns about the film. I cannot decide whether Guilty of Romance replicates the love and theft it depicts, using women’s abjection as aesthetic material, or whether it represents and criticises that structure instead. This returns us to the undecidability from the first section.

The frame

So I can turn Arnesen over now as in what I’ve said at the start. The reason I cannot decide is that the object is unsettled in its very constitution, since fascination by definition means even the person doing the projecting unsure whether he envies or despises. The analyst’s inability to decide faithfully records the phenomenon’s own inability to decide, and the two are structurally identical. This is why it recurs at every scale, in the worker, in the society, in Sono Sion, and in myself, who also cannot tell whether I am looking out of curiosity at something different or recognising myself in something shared.

Thus undecidability is scale-invariant. That looks like the everywhere trap coming back in triumph, since if every gaze is fascinated then none can be judged, however the undecidability reaches only as far as the affect. What stays unsettled is whether the fascination is envy or contempt, while the content it carries, the direction it runs and the function it serves remain decidable, and the politics there. Arnesen’s charge falls on the affect and cannot follow it onto that second ground, which is the ground from which I am still entitled to criticise. The charge of unfalsifiability is therefore the finding of this blog, and the boundary of the affect is the limit that keeps the finding from being too inclusive. I shall not close the question since I have now pulled back far enough to see my own opening inside my framing (which is another example of the return I have been describing all along).

A/N: I owe both the word ‘fascination’ and the example of Rome to a friend, who reached them from a very brief and early account of my idea. That the association should have come to anyone on so little remains startling to me, and the essay is built around what they gave me.
