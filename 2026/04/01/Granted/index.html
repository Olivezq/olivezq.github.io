好 | Granted

中文版

曾经他们胡须攒动，交错间一根指向月，一根指向日。其间天地都是他们的。

“我我我…我把女儿嫁给你！最漂亮的那个！求求你，放我一条生路…”

那样宽敞的天地，怎么会如今都没有生路。

猫咧出一条细长的嘴型，说一声”好”，终于走了。

猫说：”结婚算不算大喜事，要分情况看。比如我们。

“天宫十二生肖选举那天我等你爸等得好苦，你爸却抛下我一人，偷偷跑了，又偷偷藏进牛角里。你爸在那遥远的天宫得了第一，而我还守在人间的树洞里等着他，等着他，等着他出现，直到那比赛结果都出来，我都不敢相信选举已经结束了。蛇说你爸这是聪明，牛好脾气解释这是任何动物都有闪光点；可我要证明他们都错了。恶就应该有恶报。我说’我们明明答应一起去，你却言而无信，害我本有比赛资格却失去机会，你就这样想赢，想要名垂千古？你该如何偿还这回不来的光阴和信任？’你爸说’我把最漂亮的女儿嫁给你。’

“首先，这不足以偿还任何东西。不是说你没有价值，恰恰是因为你，你是无法衡量的，不应该被用来交换的，你也不是他的所有物。其次，你的外貌、种族、性别都和我毫无关系，你和我结婚在我看来对我没有好处，对你没有好处。你爸偏偏选了最荒谬的偿还方式。当然，他选其他的也是偿还不够的。我如何复仇也是不够的。因为我失去的也无法衡量和回来。”

“猫，可是我的鼠生还要继续。”

猫看向妻子：”哦，可是你的鼠生已经既定了，不是吗。想象一下，我在能同时杀了你、你的兄弟姐妹、你妈和你爸的情况下，你爸选择了推出谁去牺牲？接下来我要是吃了你，他要继续推谁去给他挡灾？最后棋盘上只会有我和你爸。在那之前你们就是你爸的棋子。好孩子，不要哭，我想为你解释清楚，你是不可避免的一环。而且，我饿了。比赛过后我一无所有。绝望是空洞的，占满了整个大脑，我停不下来，反复想这一切怎么发生，怎么收场。只有与食物的接触是我与世界最直接无害的联系了。”

猫残忍杀害了眼前的老鼠。他砍下她的头，嗦出脖颈的血汁，接着是手臂，一口口吞下肚腩里的器官花园，最后一饮而尽头颅里剩下的所有液体。

剪下她的胡须，撒在鼠的门前。

这是我们的天地，一根指向过去，一根指向未来。

鼠在门后隐隐听见风声。冬日的风有着刮骨寒凉的气息，递来猫的呓语——我答应过你，满足你所有愿望，你既说要生，我便让你生。

从此你将只剩下生。

English Ver.

Their whiskers once bristled in tandem, crossing in the space between, one pointing toward the moon, one toward the sun. All the world between was theirs.

“I – I – I’ll marry my daughter to you! The most beautiful one! Please, I’m begging you, spare me, let me live – “

So vast, that world had been. How is it that now there is no way out alive.

Cat stretched his mouth into a long, thin shape, said “deal,” and finally left.

Cat said: “Whether a wedding counts as a joyous occasion, that depends. Take ours.

“The day of the Heavenly Court’s great zodiac race, I waited for your father until it ached. Your father abandoned me, slipped away in secret, and hid himself inside Ox’s horn. Your father stood in that faraway celestial palace and took first place, while I was still in my hollow in the earth, waiting for him, waiting for him, waiting for him to appear, until the results were announced and I still could not believe the race was already over. Snake called your father clever; Ox, good-tempered, explained that every animal has a redeeming quality; but I would prove them all wrong. Evil should be repaid in kind. I said: we agreed to go together, and you broke your word, you robbed me of my rightful place; you wanted to win that badly, to have your name last through the ages? How will you repay the time and the trust that will never return? Your father said: I will marry my most beautiful daughter to you.

“First, this is not enough to repay anything. Not because you have no worth, but precisely because you do; you are immeasurable, you should never be exchanged, and you are not his to give. Second, your appearance, your species, your sex, none of it concerns me; this marriage does me no good and does you no good. Your father chose the most absurd form of repayment, though of course any other form would also have fallen short, and any revenge of mine would also fall short, because what I lost is equally immeasurable and equally irrecoverable.”

“Cat, but my life still has to go on.”

Cat turned to his wife: “Oh, but your life has already been decided, hasn’t it. Consider: when I could have killed you, your siblings, your mother, and your father all at once, who did your father choose to push forward? And once I have eaten you, who will he push out next to take the blow? In the end the board holds only me and your father; until then, you are all his pieces. Good child, don’t cry, I want to make this clear to you, you are an inevitable step. Also, I’m hungry. After the race I had nothing; the despair was hollow, it filled my entire skull, and I could not stop turning it over, how it all happened, how it all ends. The only contact with the world that still felt direct and harmless was the contact with food.”

Cat killed the mouse before him with savage precision. He severed her head, drew the blood from the neck in one long pull, then the arms, swallowed mouthful by mouthful the organ garden inside the belly, and drank to the last drop every liquid left in the skull.

He cut off her whiskers and scattered them at Rat’s door.

This is our world, one pointing toward the past, one toward the future.

Behind the door, Rat heard the wind faintly. The winter wind carried a bone-scraping cold, delivering Cat’s murmur – I promised to grant your every wish; you asked to live, so I let you live.

From now on, living is all you will have left.

A/N: Happy April Fools’. In the Chinese zodiac origin myth, Rat and Cat agreed to travel together to the Jade Emperor’s great race which would decide the twelve zodiac animals. Rat broke that promise and he slipped away in the night, hid inside Ox’s horn, and leapt ahead to claim first place. Cat never arrived because he was waiting for the Rat. That is why, the story goes, cats hunt rats to this day.

In the fuller version of the story, Cat later comes to collect, and Rat, desperate, offers his most beautiful daughter in marriage, and Cat eventually devours her. Here I change only the consciousness inside it. I wanted the most lucid analysis in the story to belong to the one who devours, therefore showing that seeing clearly and doing right are not the same thing.

Rat begged for a way to live and Cat said yes. The April Fools’ trick for this one is that there was no trick! Every word was true and every deal was honoured.
