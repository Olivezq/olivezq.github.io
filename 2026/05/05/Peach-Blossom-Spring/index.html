桃花源后记｜Postscript to the Peach Blossom Spring

A/N (Foreword)

I wrote this short play in May 2020, for a school stage performance on World Environment Day.

Re-reading it now, six years later and halfway through a degree that has taught me to be suspicious of exactly the kind of redemption arc this play stages… the structure surprised me. I have left the original text untouched, added an English translation alongside it, and written a longer afterword at the end that tries to think about what the play does and does not see, and what it might look like if I wrote it now.

The play is in classical Chinese pastiche for the fisherman and modern Mandarin for the two children, a register split that I have tried to preserve in translation by giving the fisherman an early-modern English voice (thee, thou, hath) and the children contemporary speech.

For readers unfamiliar with the source: Tao Yuanming’s Peach Blossom Spring (《桃花源记》, c. 421 CE) is one of the most canonical short pieces in Chinese literary history. A fisherman from Wuling follows a stream lined with peach blossoms and stumbles into a hidden valley whose inhabitants, refugees from the Qin dynasty wars, have lived in isolated peace for six hundred years. He can never find the way back and the valley becomes a byword for unreachable utopia.

This play is what happens after.

出场人物 / Cast

  • 武陵渔人 The Fisherman of Wuling
  • 少年甲 First Boy
  • 少年乙 Second Boy
  • 旁白 Narrator

脚本、导演、剧务 / Script, Director, Stage Management
郑旗 Zheng Qi

剧本 / Script

（旁白：） 武陵渔人自从桃花源一别，对当初在桃花源中度过的日子念念不忘。奈何遍寻不着归路，只得重操旧业，以捕鱼为生。

(NARRATOR.) Ever since his parting from the Peach Blossom Spring, the fisherman of Wuling has been unable to put those days from his mind. Yet though he has searched far and wide, no path will lead him back. He has returned to his old trade and lives by fishing.

年复一年，每逢春至，村中桃李盛开，渔人不免睹物思旧。这一日他乘着小舟，顺流而往，不料远远瞧见溪水尽头有一处山隘，桃花盛开，灿若云霞。

Year after year, each time spring comes and the peach and plum trees in his village burst into bloom, the sight stirs the old longing in him. On this particular day, drifting downstream in his small boat, he glimpses, far ahead at the end of the water, a narrow gorge between hills — and peach blossoms in full flower, glowing like sunset cloud.

（渔人讶道） “这！莫不正是那桃花源的入口？”（低头沉吟，又抬首四顾）”不，这似乎并不是上回去往桃花源的路径，罢了，还是去看看吧。”（说罢摇橹前行）

(FISHERMAN, astonished.) “Lo! Could this be none other than the gateway to the Peach Blossom Spring?” (He bows his head in thought, then looks about him.) “Nay — methinks ‘tis not the path I trod before. No matter; I shall go and see.” (So saying, he plies his oar onward.)

（旁白） 渔人一路只见落英缤纷。走近后，却见自那山隘中透出昏黄的光来。渔人系舟登岸，走近山隘向里张望。但见深处别有洞天。苍穹之下，有银色巨鸟呼啸而过。远处高楼鳞次栉比，密密匝匝仿佛要接到天边去。

(NARRATOR.) All along the way, the fisherman sees fallen petals drifting on the water. But as he draws nearer, a dim yellow light spills out from the gorge. He moors his boat, steps ashore, and approaches the opening to peer within. Within, another world entirely lies. Beneath the vault of heaven, silver birds of monstrous size go shrieking past. Far off, towers stand rank upon rank, packed close as if they would press their tops against the very edge of the sky.

渔人走入山隘，见近旁有两位奇装异服的少年，正蹲在溪水边用奇怪的透明容器取水。

The fisherman steps into the gorge, and there beside him crouch two strangely dressed youths at the stream’s edge, drawing water into queer transparent vessels.

（渔人上前）： “两位小兄弟，敢问此乃何处？尔等缘何作此装扮？”

(FISHERMAN, approaching.) “Good young sirs, pray tell me — what manner of place is this? And wherefore art thou clad in such strange attire?”

少年乙： “咦，你谁呀？你的装扮怎么这个样子？拍戏吗？”

SECOND BOY. “Huh? Who are you? Why are you dressed like that — are you filming something?”

“在下武陵人氏，为寻桃花源而来，未料误入宝地，却不知此为何处仙乡？竟如此光怪陆离？”

“Thy servant is a man of Wuling, come hither in search of the Peach Blossom Spring. By chance I have stumbled upon thy precious land, yet I know not what blessed realm this be — for ‘tis passing strange and wondrous beyond all telling.”

少年乙： “哇！武陵渔人长得就是这个样子啊？我们刚学过讲桃花源的课文！你没在骗我吧？”

SECOND BOY. “Whoa! So this is what the Wuling fisherman actually looks like? We literally just studied that text in class! You’re not pulling my leg, are you?”

少年甲推推眼镜： “如果你没有扯谎，那么这里对于你的时代来说，是一千多年后的未来。”

FIRST BOY (pushing up his glasses). “If you aren’t lying, then for your era, this place is more than a thousand years in the future.”

渔人大惊，惶然四顾：”啊！一千多年！小兄弟莫……莫要诓我！”

The fisherman is struck with dismay and looks wildly about him. “Alas! A thousand years! Young sir, do not — do not deceive me!”

少年乙掐指： “等等等等，让我来算算。”

SECOND BOY (counting on his fingers). “Hold on, hold on, let me work it out.”

少年乙背诵《朝代歌》（一边狐疑地围绕着渔人转悠，一边摇头晃脑有节奏地背诵）

(Second Boy recites the Dynasty Song, circling the fisherman with a sceptical air, head bobbing in rhythm.)

夏商周，春秋战。
秦朝以后是两汉，
三国两晋南北朝。
隋唐五代宋辽夏，
金元明清二十朝。

“Xia, Shang, Zhou — Spring and Autumn, Warring States.
After the Qin came the two Han.
Three Kingdoms, two Jins, Northern and Southern Courts.
Sui, Tang, Five Dynasties, Song, Liao, Xia,
Jin, Yuan, Ming, Qing — twenty dynasties in all.”

少年乙一拍大腿：”哎哟！算算从东晋到现在，您可是一千六百多年前的古人了。”（好奇地上下打量渔人）”这么说来，您本人可不就是个活的老古董了么？”（两眼闪闪发光）

Second Boy slaps his thigh. “Aiyo! Counting from the Eastern Jin to now, you’d be a man from over sixteen hundred years ago.” (He looks the fisherman up and down with frank curiosity.) “Which means, you’re basically a living antique!” (His eyes glitter.)

少年甲轻推少年乙，提醒他注意礼貌： “什么老古董，得尊称一声’人瑞爷爷’。”

FIRST BOY (nudging Second Boy and reminding him of his manners). “Don’t say ‘antique.’ Show some respect, call him ‘Venerable Grandfather.’”

渔人心神大震，张望四周，对两人颔首道：”二位所言不虚。若非未来，何来此等闻所未闻之景！只是，我原本自碧水青天之处而来，为何此地……”（手指颤抖着指向远处，面露惊讶）”却昏暗晦暝，竟似有雾霭遮天蔽日？就这？”

The fisherman, deeply shaken, casts his gaze about and bows his head to them. “What ye twain say must be true. Were it not the future, whence such sights as I have never beheld nor heard of? Yet — I came hither from a land of clear waters and azure skies. Why is it that here…” (With trembling finger he points into the distance, his face full of wonder.) “…all is dim and obscure, as though a fog hath veiled the very sun? Is this all there is?”

少年甲叹息： “唉，说起来，这还是人类的过错。百年来，人类社会科技和文明高速发展，人们不断地向大自然索取资源、大量使用无法降解的塑料制品、过量排放污水和热能，导致海平面上升、极地冰川融化、天空上方的臭氧层也受到波及，空气质量混浊，自然也就看不到碧水青天的景色了。”

FIRST BOY (sighing). “Ah, to tell the truth, this is humankind’s own doing. Over the past hundred years, our science and civilisation have advanced at breakneck speed. People have taken endlessly from nature, used vast quantities of plastics that will not decompose, discharged sewage and waste heat without restraint. The seas have risen; the polar ice melts; even the ozone layer overhead is wounded. The air has grown thick and foul. Of course one can no longer see clear waters and azure skies.”

渔人问： “敢问二位在此所为何事？”

FISHERMAN. “And what, pray, art thou twain doing in this place?”

少年乙举起手中的采集瓶： “我们正在采集实验数据，从自然水域中取得天然水样本，由我们班的博士级科学怪人进行分析。”（指向少年甲）

SECOND BOY (holding up his collection bottle). “We’re gathering experimental data — taking samples from natural waterways for analysis. Our class’s resident mad scientist here does the actual lab work.” (He points at First Boy.)

少年甲： “低调、低调！”（风采的动作）”从我们对这条小溪的观测结果来看，污染情况很严重。工业排放的气体和废水蒸发形成酸雨，落在附近的山坡上。”（指着附近）”你看，酸雨所经之处，植被凋零，而以草木为生的虫蚁与动物，数目也大为减少。这些酸雨和工业废水汇集到小溪中，破坏了自然水域中的生态平衡，致使水体混沌，鱼虾、微生物和藻类无法生存。”

FIRST BOY. “Modest, modest!” (Strikes a poised attitude.) “From our observations of this stream, the pollution is severe. Industrial emissions and evaporated wastewater form acid rain, which falls on the surrounding hillsides.” (He points around them.) “Look — wherever the acid rain has fallen, the vegetation withers, and the insects and animals that depend on plants have dwindled in number. This acid rain and industrial effluent drain into the stream, breaking the ecological balance of the natural water. The water grows turbid, and fish, shrimp, microorganisms, and algae can no longer survive.”

渔人： “却不知这虫蚁与动物数目、又或鱼虾，竟与环境有关？”

FISHERMAN. “I knew not that the numbers of insects and beasts, of fish and shrimp, were thus bound up with the state of their dwelling-place.”

少年乙： “当然有关啦。那些看似不起眼的小草和小昆虫，它们正是生物多样性的基础。由于人类生活领地的的扩大，许多物种绝迹。相比起您所生活的晋代，我们这个时代的生物种类已是相当贫乏。生物种类的缺失，进一步影响到生态、气候、地质结构，如果继续恶性循环下去，怕是这个世界再不能见到朗朗乾坤，碧水青天了。”（叹息）

SECOND BOY. “Of course they are! Those little plants and tiny insects that look so ordinary — they are the foundation of biodiversity. As humans have spread further and further into the wild, many species have gone extinct. Compared to your Jin dynasty, the variety of life in our time is already deeply impoverished. And the loss of species feeds back into climate, ecosystems, geological structures. If this vicious cycle continues, this world may never see bright heavens and clear waters again.” (He sighs.)

渔人： “二位可有解决之道？”

FISHERMAN. “Have ye twain any remedy?”

少年乙： “办法倒是有，但……此事并非一人能为啊。”

SECOND BOY. “There is, but… this isn’t something one person can do alone.”

渔人： “此话怎讲？”

FISHERMAN. “How meanest thou?”

少年甲（双手背后，踱步上前，面向观众）： “环境保护之道，在于众志成城。唯有每一位地球居民都身体力行地爱护环境、心存拯救地球的善念、不滥用自然资源、爱护野生动物、使用可降解材料，世界才会越来越好。”

FIRST BOY (hands clasped behind his back, pacing forward to face the audience). “The way of environmental protection lies in the union of all wills. Only when every inhabitant of this Earth, in their own person, cherishes the environment, holds in their heart the wish to save the planet, refuses to squander natural resources, protects wild creatures, and uses biodegradable materials, only then will the world grow better.”

少年乙问渔人： “嘿！对了，这位老祖宗……不不，这位人瑞爷爷，您还要回到晋代吗？”

SECOND BOY (turning to the fisherman). “Hey — by the way, ancient ancestor — I mean, Venerable Grandfather — do you still want to go back to the Jin dynasty?”

渔人回首张望来处，竟瞧不见来时路：”啊！我来时的那个山隘呢！”（着急地四处张望寻找）

The fisherman turns to look back the way he came but the path is gone. “Lo! The gorge whence I came, where is it?” (He casts about anxiously, searching.)

少年甲边思索边说： “如果您果真是来自晋代。那么您出现在这里的唯一通道就只有’时空虫洞’。根据全息原理，所有在第五维的事件可以变换为在其它四维的事件，引力与它弯曲时空的能力来自于量子纠缠。在纠缠粒子被生成的同时，时空虫洞也被生成……”（这一段语速稍快，念念有词）

FIRST BOY (speaking thoughtfully). “If you truly come from the Jin, then the only possible passage by which you arrived here is a ‘spacetime wormhole.’ According to the holographic principle, all events in the fifth dimension can be transformed into events in the other four dimensions, and gravity together with its capacity to curve spacetime arises from quantum entanglement. At the moment entangled particles are produced, a spacetime wormhole is also produced…” (He delivers this rapidly, half to himself.)

渔人摇头： “小兄弟所言，在下完全听不懂。”

FISHERMAN (shaking his head). “Of what the young sir speaks, thy servant comprehendeth not a word.”

少年乙： “他的意思是啊，量子纠缠是瞬间并且偶然发生的，时空虫洞不会随时存在。很遗憾您可能回不去了，得留在这儿。”

SECOND BOY. “What he means is, quantum entanglement happens in an instant, and by chance. The wormhole isn’t always there. Unfortunately, you probably can’t go back. You’ll have to stay here.”

渔人如释重负： “哦，那不打紧。自上回离开桃花源，在下悔不当初，错失仙缘。如今机缘巧合来到此地，不如在此好好过活。只是尚有一事未明，需向二位请教。”（抬手作揖）

FISHERMAN (with an air of relief). “Oh, that mattereth not. Since I last departed the Peach Blossom Spring, I have rued the day, having lost a divine fortune through mine own folly. Now, by some happy chance, I have come hither — better, then, that I make my dwelling here. Yet there is one thing still that puzzles me, and I would beg of ye an answer.” (He raises his hands and bows.)

少年甲少年乙回礼同答： “请说。”

FIRST BOY and SECOND BOY (returning the bow together). “Please ask.”

渔人： “在下前回误入之境被称为’桃花源’。那么，现下你我所在之境，当如何称呼？”（略思索）”莫非是……莫非是……’污染源’？”

FISHERMAN. “The realm into which I once strayed was called the Peach Blossom Spring — Tao Hua Yuan. What then, pray, is the name of this realm wherein we three now stand?” (He pauses in thought.) “Could it be… could it be… the Pollution Spring — the Wu Ran Yuan?”

少年甲少年乙相视大笑： “不。我们有信心，将我们这个世界修复成’桃花源’！”

FIRST BOY and SECOND BOY (exchanging a look and bursting into laughter). “No! We have faith, that we shall mend this world of ours back into a Peach Blossom Spring!”

三人至台前：”关爱自然，刻不容缓！”

(All three step forward to the front of the stage.) “To cherish nature, there is not a moment to lose!”

【剧终】全体演职员鞠躬道谢，退场。

[END.] The full company bows in thanks and exits.

A/N (Afterword): Reading This Six Years On

The structural choice is the strongest thing in it. The original Peach Blossom Spring is built around a movement from a debased present (the wars of the late Eastern Jin) into a recovered ideal past (a community of Qin refugees still living the agrarian peace of six centuries earlier). The fisherman crosses from the corrupted now into the preserved then. Postscript inverts this: the fisherman, already inhabiting what Tao’s text codes as the lost ideal, crosses into the corrupted future. The pastoral past now looks back at industrial modernity and finds it sick. This is an ironic, almost elegiac structural move, and it does real work, it stages climate degradation as something a pre-modern observer would find legible as harm, which is itself a quiet rebuke to the modernist story that progress has been a uniform improvement.

The register split between classical Chinese pastiche and modern Mandarin is the play’s other genuine craft achievement. The fisherman’s speech indexes a knowledge-world: bi shui qing tian (clear water and azure skies) is a literary topos, freighted with poetic use. When he asks why the sky is dim, he is registering a loss of a cosmological category. The boys’ speech, by contrast, is the diction of the school environmental curriculum: biodiversity, ecological balance, acid rain. The two characters speak from inside two different epistemic communities.

Here is where six years of reading STS and decolonial environmental thought changes what I notice.

The framing is unreflectively diffusionist. The two children are positioned as scientific knowledge-bearers and the fisherman as a recipient of explanation. He asks; they explain. This reproduces what Jasanoff would call a deficit model of public understanding: the layperson (here, the historical person) is empty, and the scientifically literate filler-in-the-blank is the source of true knowing. But the fisherman of Wuling is not epistemically empty. He is a person whose livelihood depends on detailed observational knowledge of a watershed, fish behaviour, seasonal cycles, water clarity, vegetation along banks. He would, in fact, be among the first to register an ecological wrongness in the boys’ polluted stream, and probably to do so more acutely and more quickly than they can with their sample bottles.

The pastoral ideal is left unproblematised. Postscript mobilises the Peach Blossom Spring as a pre-political baseline of ecological health that modernity has fallen away from, and that we should “repair the world back into.” But the Tao Hua Yuan is not actually an ecological vision; it is a refugee fantasy of a community that has withdrawn from history. Its “harmony with nature” is a side-effect of low population density and pre-industrial productive technology, not an intentional environmental ethic. To read it as a sustainability template is to perform what Hulme has called the moralisation of climate, the conversion of a complex socio-technical situation into a fall-and-redemption narrative.

Co-production is missing. Following Jasanoff, the production of scientific knowledge and the production of social order are mutually constitutive: who counts as a knower, what counts as an observation, which forms of evidence are legible, and they are made together with the political and institutional arrangements that authorise them. The boys’ sample bottle is an artefact of a specific epistemic regime (laboratory analysis, instrumented quantification, the school science curriculum). The fisherman’s idiom of bi shui qing tian is an artefact of another (literary memory, observation embedded in classical poetics). The play has both regimes on stage and could have staged the encounter between them as the actual drama. Instead it lets the modern regime narrate the encounter and reduces the older one to a figure of innocence.

The fisherman’s affect is wrong. He arrives in a polluted future and accepts staying with relief. This makes no sense for the character the original text gives us, a man defined by not being able to find his way back to a beloved place.
