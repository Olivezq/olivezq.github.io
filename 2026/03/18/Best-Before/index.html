Introducing "Best Before"

Update (2026-03-19): So I created another website called empIRe, since my friend said they wanted to have something similar for International Relations students as well.

So I built a supermarket.

Best Before is a website about expiration, of news, of attention, of knowledge, of the planet. It borrows the visual language of a grocery store. The Shelf pulls real-time environmental and climate news from RSS feeds (Mongabay, Grist, Global Voices, Yale E360, The Conversation), and presents them as reduced-to-clear products.

The Compost section is where things go to decompose into something useful. It is an environmental humanities resource hub organised as a supermarket floor plan. Each aisle holds a different category: academic papers and journals (auto-updating via RSS from Environmental Humanities, The Anthropocene Review, Cultural Geographies, Journal of Political Ecology), films, artworks and fashion, poetry and writing. There is also a Random Refresh aisle that gives you a writing prompt, a challenge, a fact, or a reading recommendation each time you visit, to learn whatever you land on.

Two other things are in Compost, which are adapted from my previous posts on May 3 and May 4 2025. The Anthropocene Calendar documents what happened on each calendar date during the Anthropocene, drawn from published sources across climate, ecology, and socio-technical history. And the Anthropocene Lottery is a lot-drawing ritual I wrote, with twenty possible endings for the human species, with one true ending. Some lines are adapted from the translated writings of Shi Tiesheng and it is not meant as prophecy.

The Label page is the back of the product. A single information sticker at the centre of a white page introducing this website.

The whole thing was built in a day with Lovable, with Claude handling the prompt engineering. The site’s favicon is a dandelion, a plant that looks like a flower but is usually pulled out as a weed, never allowed in the garden.
