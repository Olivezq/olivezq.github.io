夏花 | Summer Flower

中文版

床铺和天花板的距离太近了。陈瑞霖戴上眼镜，一层蚊帐后，白墙上斑纹点点。尖刺不足以划破喉咙，比锉平的指甲还无害。玫瑰的头开始下垂，原来它也有和瑞蓝西村的小孩一样的扁头。花瓣边缘沾上黑边，和老电影的白色噪点。花内包裹廉价酒店的大红沙发皮，陈瑞霖两指拔开，然而只是看见更嫩、更多的褶皱。他停下动作，双手握住离沙沙的叶子最远的花柄。玫瑰在胸前。

一旁的风扇灰绿地伫立。他突然想找一捆绳子，把自己拴在上面，再从床上跳下。高中时设想过类似的，如果旋转的风扇掉下，会不会正好把脑袋切掉？笔在右手里不停转动。清楚记得第一节语文课就捏过扁平的小钱包了，于是一心只想回家，走过灯红酒绿的步行街。关门，门背后总挂一块“金榜题名”的冰箱贴。”你摔门给谁看的，啊？““吃饭吧，霖子，我给你下点面条子喝吧。”“怎么了啊，霖子，怎么光吃面不吃菜啊？”“哎呀爱吃吃不吃回去。“”怎么了这是，哭了？没考好？“

老妈坐在电视机前，身体煞白。老爸的脸在灯下极为恐怖，眼下垂挂两颗漆黑的阴影，附着在眼袋上。他们都看着他。

“答应考好就给我买手机…又说话不算数。“他埋头挑起一缕面。

“你还给我提这个手机，啊？”“别、别骂霖子了。”“你知不知道现在经济不景气？！你妈不工作在家专门养你，我拼死拼活的我赚钱容易啊，还被你甩上脸色了…都是让人惯的！“

筷子飞到天上，老妈老爸开始说悄悄话。“孩子现在上学都累…““你看看那月末工资单…”

三年过去，高考刚好到黄昌大学的分数线。他离开瑞蓝。初到的烈日炎炎，在更大更空洞的黄昌上奔跑。陈瑞霖想起在议论文里经常引用的“生如夏花之绚烂”。

如果陈瑞霖知道如今境地，他绝对不会那天，开学第二周，同意加张馨云的微信。不，他连食堂都不会去。张馨云在食堂偷拍他的那张，已经被贴在记忆的耻辱柱最顶端了。

大二，家里开始给不起足够生活费，于是导员给了他一份代课的工作。当然是赚多少花多少。期末周没课可代了，好歹招生会邀请了他，给点酬劳，说是因为他德智体美劳全面发展。张馨云说：“就是你长得帅。你看你绩点才多少。”

“我还是篮球队的。”

“哎呀，我的小猫可爱吧，”张馨云转过屏幕，“它是银渐层和蓝猫杂交。”

“银渐层和蓝猫很贵的。”

“很贵吗？”

他突然不敢说话了。仓鼠的身影一闪而过，然而似乎毫无联系。

“不过是我小姨家的。”她不知想了什么。他为解读不出她的反应而更加害怕。

张馨云曾送继弟一只仓鼠。继弟并不喜欢，丢给姨妈养。猫是张馨云觉得可爱就借来的，她老说没钱，所以陈瑞霖从来不问到底买的哪种猫粮猫砂。爱一条生命，就要给它最好的，见不得它有受伤的可能，不是吗？然而他无法说出这句话。一旦说出，必然要质疑：那他没有给张馨云最好的，是不爱她吗；父母打骂他，是不爱他吗；如果爱，那为何知道疼痛还要捅破那层膜，为何没有不停工作，把钱都倾注她身上？

约会好遥远。“崔丽娥每次约会都是她对象买单，平时水果奶茶都是他花钱，他家里知道还给他恋爱补贴。然后每个月，他还寄一箱大快递，里面垫拉菲草，好多她喜欢吃的零食和衣服、盲盒。“

“哇。”他见过每次张馨云都要发过来的视频。崔丽娥像钻进稻草窝的小白狗似的。她高悬的皎洁，配上张馨云在一旁的黏腻声调，都让他不忍心地眯起眼。

“哼，我也想要，好不好嘛。人家谈女朋友都是当个宝捧在心上。我突然发现我这周给你点外卖都花了五十，难道我就很有钱吗？我给你点二十的奶茶，你连两块钱的冰淇淋都不能给我买。办法总是有的，只不过你根本没想着给我，我也明明说了自己想吃。你在你室友面前对我有哪一点能看出来我是你对象？“

“我买不起…“

“还有学习一定要我带着你学，你上一门已经考差了，这一门依然没有状态。这个游戏打完了是会有下一个游戏的，你还记得大一的时候怎么跟我说的，你说你不爱玩游戏，只偶尔跟室友打，我理解游戏是维持室友关系的一种方式，但还有不少游戏你都是自己在玩吧，之前说什么有我在时游戏都不重要，现在慢慢在我面前就是打游戏，我们一起上课是可以一句话都不说的，我们俩之间夹了一个崔丽娥你也不会想办法坐过来，如果是我的话我一定会去坐你身边。“

“崔丽娥跟我说她也想做但是她对象一直没同意，觉得太早了，我现在想来我当时才19岁，HPV也没打，你为什么就能进去？所以这些你有上网好好看过吗？我只是飞机杯吧？之前还说什么从来不看AV，完全骗人的，男人的嘴骗人的鬼我现在是发现了，刚谈上的时候甜言蜜语说的有多好，以后打脸就有多响。”

可是，可是。当时不是你追问我好几周，我才答应的吗？

“背心不用换货了，直接退掉吧，我觉得还是穿睡衣舒服。我给你买礼物很少问你要不要，因为我是听你说过什么就给你买，你给我买的礼物是我跟你强调过我想要，除了几块钱的小礼物。你在你兄弟面前有给足我安全感吗？我跟你撒娇求你你理我吗？我就不尴尬吗？在你的观念里就是有外人在的时候我就不是你女朋友而是女生朋友了。“

“我没有呀。”

“你还能找到哪个女生肯跟你吃饭平摊，还在一起半年就跟你做，主动给你买礼物点外卖，还可以跟你室友一起打球打牌。再说学习，你的诊断书有多干净你自己知道，病生听过一节课嘛？我让你上课不听的话也可以自己看网课，你说你课后再看，你有及时看吗我请问？陈瑞霖我感觉你跟我刚认识你的时候不一样了。也许所有的感情都是这样吧，故事的开头总是这样适逢其会猝不及防，故事的结尾总是这样花开两朵天各一方。“

张馨云最后引用同样在议论文里反复出现的一句，起身摔门而去。

陈瑞霖知道她下午要去讨导员要校园拍摄欠的工资。他数数钱，决定去买花。依然是玫瑰，给她一个惊喜。

他来到导员办公室门前。张馨云还在里面，竟然传来抽泣。

“对不起，我、我其实有抑郁症…我妈妈在我高一的时候就去世了…爸爸之后就一直在换女朋友谈，还给我生了一个弟弟…我真的很需要这笔钱…“

她咆哮而出，门砸到他手肘，玫瑰从脱力的手中坠落。她没有看到门后的他，反而撞上走廊另一头的崔丽娥。她白玉兰般的身姿笼罩张馨云，素净的藕臂环住她，关切地拥哭哭啼啼的她走上楼梯。张馨云无非又在对崔丽娥添油加醋。导员追上去，叹一口气黑着脸给出两百。

陈瑞霖低头，玫瑰像爆炸了似的。一滩烂肉。

回到宿舍，打开手机，沉浸在游戏中，可以让他暂时忘记这些。不知不觉和室友玩到很晚。估计第二天上课，又听不懂老师讲的啥。他想睡觉，但是张馨云一直在怪他没听课。是他不想听吗？他是听不动了。

他躺在床上，怀里是唯一存活的一支玫瑰。他侧头看向风扇。

老师，你所说的夏花究竟是什么？

English ver.

The bunk was too close to the ceiling. Chen Ruilin put on his glasses; behind a layer of mosquito net, the white wall was dappled with specks. The thorns weren’t enough to slit a throat, more harmless than filed-down fingernails. The rose’s head began to droop; so it too had a flat head, just like the children of Ruilan’s West Village. The petal edges had taken on black rims, like the white noise of old films. Inside the flower was wrapped the crimson sofa-leather of a cheap hotel; Chen Ruilin pried it apart with two fingers, but only saw more, tenderer folds. He stopped, both hands gripping the stem where it ran farthest from the rustling leaves. The rose against his chest.

Off to the side the fan stood in grayish green. He suddenly wanted to find a length of rope, tie himself to it, and jump down from the bed. In high school he’d imagined something similar — if the spinning fan came down, would it slice his head off just right? The pen kept spinning in his right hand. He remembered clearly that by the very first Chinese lesson he’d already squeezed that flat little wallet, and so wanted nothing but to go home, walking past the gaudy, neon-lit pedestrian street. Shut the door; behind the door there always hung a “May Your Name Top the Golden List” fridge magnet. “Who’re you slamming the door for, huh?” “Eat, Lin-zi, I’ll boil you some noodles to slurp.” “What’s wrong, Lin-zi, why only noodles, no vegetables?” “Aiya, eat it if you want, if not then go back.” “What’s this now, crying? Didn’t do well on the test?”

Mom sat in front of the TV, her body deathly white. Dad’s face under the light was utterly terrifying, two pitch-black shadows hanging below his eyes, clinging to the eye-bags. They were all looking at him.

“You promised if I did well you’d buy me a phone… and went back on your word again.” He bent his head and lifted a strand of noodles.

“You’re still bringing up about this, huh?” “Don’t — don’t scold Lin-zi.” “Do you not know the economy’s in a slump right now?! Your mom doesn’t work, she stays home just to raise you, I work myself to death, you think making money’s easy? — and I get attitude thrown in my face… it’s all from being spoiled!”

The chopsticks flew up into the air; Mom and Dad started whispering. “Kids these days are so worn out from school…” “Just look at that end-of-month pay slip…”

Three years passed; his gaokao score landed just on the cutoff for Huangchang University. He left Ruilan. The blazing sun of his arrival, running across a Huangchang larger and more hollow. Chen Ruilin thought of the line he’d so often quoted in his argumentative essays: “Let life be splendid like summer flowers.”

If Chen Ruilin had known the state he’d be in now, he absolutely would not have, that day, the second week of the semester, agreed to add Zhang Xinyun on WeChat. No, he wouldn’t even have gone to the canteen. The photo Zhang Xinyun secretly took of him in the canteen had already been nailed to the very top of memory’s pillory.

Sophomore year, his family began to be unable to give him enough living expenses, so the counselor got him a substitute-teaching job. Whatever he earned, of course, he spent. During finals week there were no classes left to cover, but at least the recruitment fair invited him, paid a little, said it was because he was all-round developed in morals, intellect, body, beauty, and labor. Zhang Xinyun said: “It’s just that you’re good-looking. Look how low your GPA is.”

“I’m on the basketball team too.”

“Aiya, isn’t my kitty cute,” Zhang Xinyun turned the screen around, “a cross between a silver shaded and a British Blue.”

“Silver shadeds and British Blues are expensive.”

“Are they expensive?”

He suddenly didn’t dare speak. The image of a hamster flashed past, though it seemed to have no connection.

“It’s only my aunt’s, anyway.” She was thinking who knows what. That he couldn’t decode her reaction frightened him all the more.

Zhang Xinyun had once given her stepbrother a hamster. The stepbrother didn’t like it and dumped it on their aunt to raise. The cat she’d borrowed just because she found it cute; she was always saying she had no money, so Chen Ruilin never asked what kind of cat food or litter she actually bought. To love a living thing is to give it the best, to be unable to bear the chance of its being hurt, isn’t it? But he couldn’t say this. Once said, it would inevitably raise the question: then his not giving Zhang Xinyun the best, was that not loving her; his parents beating and scolding him, was that not loving him; if it was love, then why, knowing the pain, still break through that membrane, why not work without stopping and pour all his money onto her?

Dating felt so far away. “Cui Li’e, every date, her boyfriend foots the bill; the fruit, the milk tea, day to day, he pays for all of it; his family knows and even gives him a dating allowance. And then, every month, he ships a big parcel, raffia padding inside, full of the snacks she likes and clothes, blind boxes.”

“Wow.” He’d seen the videos Zhang Xinyun forwarded him every single time. Cui Li’e was like a little white dog burrowing into a straw nest. Her luminous whiteness, suspended on high, paired with Zhang Xinyun’s clingy, cloying tone beside it, made him narrow his eyes, unable to bear it.

“Hmph, I want that too, okay? Other people, when they’ve got a girlfriend, hold her like a treasure in their heart. I suddenly realized I spent fifty just ordering you takeout this week, is it like I’m rich? I order you twenty-yuan milk tea, and you can’t even buy me a two-yuan ice cream. There’s always a way; it’s just that you never even think of me, when I clearly said I wanted some. In front of your roommates, what about you shows in any way that I’m your girlfriend?”

“I can’t afford it…”

“And studying, you always need me to carry you through it. You already did badly in the last course, and you’re still in no state for this one. When this game’s over there’ll be a next game; do you remember what you told me freshman year? You said you didn’t like games, only played with your roommates now and then. I understand games are a way to keep up with roommates, but there are plenty of games you play on your own too, right? You used to say things like, when I’m around, games don’t matter at all; now, little by little, right in front of me it’s just games. The two of us can sit through a whole class together without a single word. With a Cui Li’e wedged between the two of us, you still won’t find a way to move over and sit closer, if it were me, I’d definitely go and sit by your side.”

“Cui Li’e told me she wants to do it too, but her boyfriend never agreed, thought it was too early. Now that I think about it, I was only nineteen then, hadn’t even gotten the HPV shot, so why was it fine for you to go in? Have you actually looked any of this up online? Am I just a fleshlight? You even said you never watched porn, a complete lie. A man’s mouth, the lying devil — I’ve figured it out now: as sweet as the sweet talk was when we first got together, that’s how loud the slap to the face comes later.”

But, but. Wasn’t it you who pressed me for weeks back then, before I agreed?

“Don’t bother exchanging the tank top, just return it; I think pajamas are more comfortable anyway. When I buy you presents I rarely ask whether you want them, because I buy based on whatever you’ve mentioned; the presents you buy me are ones I’ve had to stress to you that I want, except for the few-yuan trinkets. In front of your buddies, have you ever given me enough sense of security? When I act sweet and beg you, do you even pay me any mind? Am I not embarrassed too? In your way of thinking, the moment there are outsiders around, I stop being your girlfriend and become a female friend.”

“I didn’t, though.”

“What other girl could you ever find who’d split the bill with you, who’d sleep with you after just half a year together, who’d buy you presents and order you takeout on her own, who could even play ball and cards with your roommates? And as for studying, you know yourself how clean your diagnostic report is; did you sit through even one Pathophysiology class? When I tell you not to listen in class you could at least watch the online lectures yourself; you said you’d watch them after class, and I ask you, did you watch them in time? Chen Ruilin, I feel you’re not the same as when I first met you. Maybe all relationships are like this: the beginning of the story is always like this, a chance encounter, caught off guard; the ending of the story is always like this, two flowers in bloom, each to its own far corner of the sky.”

Zhang Xinyun, in closing, quoted a line that likewise appeared again and again in argumentative essays, then stood up and slammed the door on her way out.

Chen Ruilin knew that in the afternoon she was going to the counselor to demand the wages owed her for the campus photoshoot. He counted his money and decided to go buy flowers. Roses, as ever — to give her a surprise.

He came to the door of the counselor’s office. Zhang Xinyun was still inside, and — of all things — sobbing came from within.

“I’m sorry, I — I actually have depression… my mom passed away back when I was in my first year of high school… after that my dad just kept dating one girlfriend after another, and even had a baby brother for me… I really need this money…”

She came roaring out; the door smashed into his elbow, and the rose dropped from his strengthless hand. She didn’t see him behind the door, instead she ran into Cui Li’e at the other end of the corridor. Cui Li’e’s white-magnolia figure loomed over Zhang Xinyun, her pale, lotus-root arms encircling her, and with concern she held the sniveling, weeping girl up the stairs. No doubt Zhang Xinyun was once again laying it on thick for Cui Li’e. The counselor caught up, let out a sigh, and with a darkened face handed over two hundred.

Chen Ruilin lowered his head; the rose looked as if it had exploded. A puddle of rotten flesh.

Back in the dorm, he opened his phone and sank into a game, which let him forget all this for a while. Without realizing it, he played with his roommates until late. Tomorrow in class, presumably, he’d again not understand a thing the teacher said. He wanted to sleep, but Zhang Xinyun kept blaming him for not listening in class. Was it that he didn’t want to listen? It was that he no longer had it in him to listen.

He lay on the bed, the one surviving rose in his arms. He turned his head and looked at the fan.

Teacher, what exactly is this summer flower you spoke of?

A/N

这篇和《绿》同属一个宇宙，毕竟吕缕和陈瑞霖都来自瑞蓝。并且依然是从朋友那里听来的这个故事，进行了改编。而对崔丽娥的描写的灵感来源于三岛由纪夫。顺带一提，我上个月写的《人和他的撒旦》中，付李其实毕业论文研究的就是瑞蓝，然而这是更加冗长的一个故事了。《人和他的撒旦》的篇幅过长，并且因为是第一部小说，我自觉水平不高，所以到现在都没英文翻译。

这篇我个人觉得好品的几点：

张馨云是为了博取导员的关心，才通过利用母亲的死来架起导员，让其不得不给她工资，出去和崔丽娥也是卖惨。这是真正压垮陈瑞霖的稻草，他不得不面对张馨云是一个毫无道德底线和分寸感的人，他之前一直都不愿深思张馨云对动物的不尊重、视生命如鸿毛的态度，如今他不得不面对了，她确实是连母亲的去世都要利用的人。而崔丽娥“钻进稻草窝的小白狗”和”白玉兰般的身姿……素净的藕臂”都是张瑞霖的视角，这里是说陈瑞霖和张馨云一样，都必不可免拿自己感情和其他人的比较；把丽娥看作“小白狗”，说明他在觉得她可爱的同时是在把她当作更低位的东西观赏的，“白玉兰”“藕臂”则就是单纯凝视她的外貌。就是说，他也并非全心全意喜欢张馨云，也并非只是“精神上的巨人”。

陈瑞霖永远无法达到“生如夏花”的那种高度，他永远不知道那是什么，因为他被生活的这些琐事麻痹了，如同温水煮青蛙。就像他不愿意承认张馨云是毫无底线的人，他也无法承认自己也是一个麻痹自我的人，他将永远看着风扇幻想死亡，然而第二天继续起床，听不懂课，被女朋友骂，如此捆绑、没有勇气地走过自己一生。

然而我欢迎各种其他解读，毕竟我不应给予情感。

This piece belongs to the same universe as Green, since both Lü Lü and Chen Ruilin come from Ruilan. And, as before, this is a story I heard from a friend and then adapted. The inspiration for the depiction of Cui Li’e comes from Yukio Mishima. Incidentally, in People and Their Satan, which I wrote last month, Fu Li’s thesis actually researches Ruilan, but that’s a far longer story. People and Their Satan runs too long, and since it’s my first novel I don’t think much of my own level, so to this day there’s no English translation of it.

A few things I personally find worth savoring in this piece:

Zhang Xinyun, in order to win the counselor’s sympathy, exploits her mother’s death to corner the counselor, leaving him no choice but to hand over her wages; going off with Cui Li’e is likewise a play for pity. This is the true last straw that breaks Chen Ruilin, he is forced to face that Zhang Xinyun is a person without any moral bottom line or sense of proportion. He had always been unwilling to think hard about Zhang Xinyun’s disrespect for animals, her treatment of life as light as a feather; now he has no choice but to face it, that she really is someone who would exploit even her own mother’s death. And Cui Li’e as “a little white dog burrowing into a straw nest” and as the “white-magnolia figure… pale, lotus-root arms” are both Chen Ruilin’s point of view; the point here is that Chen Ruilin, just like Zhang Xinyun, inevitably compares his own relationship against other people’s. Seeing Li’e as a “little white dog” shows that even as he finds her cute he is appraising her as something lower in position; “white magnolia” and “lotus-root arms” are simply a gaze fixed on her looks. That is to say, he does not wholeheartedly love Zhang Xinyun either, nor is he merely a “giant of the spirit.”

Chen Ruilin can never reach the height of “life splendid like summer flowers”; he will never know what it is, because he has been numbed by these trivialities of living, like a frog in slowly warming water. Just as he is unwilling to admit that Zhang Xinyun is a person without any bottom line, he is also unable to admit that he himself is a person who numbs himself; he will forever stare at the fan and fantasize about death, yet get up again the next day, fail to understand his classes, get scolded by his girlfriend, and walk through his entire life bound up like this, without courage.

That said, I welcome all manner of other readings, after all, I shouldn’t be supplying emotions.
